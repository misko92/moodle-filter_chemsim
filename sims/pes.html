Photoelectron Spectroscopy (PES)

Tap a peak to learn about it. The energy axis is logarithmic so that core and valence peaks fit on one chart, and energy increases to the left.
In PES, high-energy photons knock electrons out of atoms. The detector measures each electron's kinetic energy (KE). The energy needed to remove it is what is left over: IE = hν − KE. Every electron counted adds to the peak for its subshell, so the peak heights end up showing how many electrons are in each subshell.
Idealized: every electron is equally likely to be knocked out, and the photon energy is 500 MJ/mol (X-rays), enough to remove any electron in these atoms. Numbers above the peaks are the electrons counted so far.
